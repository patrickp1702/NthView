Incident Response in IT and OT

How enterprises detect, contain, recover from and report cybersecurity incidents, and why the playbook changes when the systems run factories, pipelines and power grids.

For: Developers, IT staff and managers who want an enterprise-level picture of incident response; no security background needed.

Glossary

Chain of custody ch. 3
The record of who held a piece of evidence, when, and what they did with it, so it holds up later.
Containment ch. 3
Stopping an incident from spreading while keeping what you need to investigate and recover.
Incident response ch. 2
The organised way an organisation detects, contains, removes and recovers from a security incident, then learns from it.
Notification clock ch. 5
A legal deadline to report an incident to a regulator or affected people, starting when you become aware of it.
OT ch. 2
Operational technology: the systems that control physical processes in plants, pipelines and grids.
Purdue levels ch. 4
A layered model of a plant network, from field devices at the bottom to business IT at the top, with a DMZ between.
Safety instrumented system ch. 4
Independent equipment that forces a process to a safe state when limits are crossed, regardless of other systems.
Tabletop exercise ch. 2
A rehearsal of an incident around a table, following the playbook without touching real systems.
